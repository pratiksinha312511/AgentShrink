Why I Stopped Using Fancy Productivity Tools (And Just Use a Notepad Now)

Okay, I'll admit it. I spent three years trying every productivity app on the planet. Notion. Obsidian. Roam Research. Linear. Todoist. I had a system for my system. I had tags for my tags. It was a mess.

Here's what nobody tells you about productivity tools: they become the work. You spend more time organizing your tasks than actually doing them. That's not productivity. That's procrastination with a beautiful UI.

So last January, I rage-quit all of it. Opened a plain text file on my desktop. Named it "today.txt". That was it.

And honestly? Best decision I've made in years.

Here's why it works for me. There's zero friction. No app to open, no workspace to load, no "getting started" tutorial. It's just a file. I type. I delete. Done.

I also stopped pretending I could plan more than one day ahead. Spoiler: I can't. Neither can you, probably. Life is chaotic. Clients cancel. Servers go down. Your dog eats something weird and you're at the vet at 2pm. No Notion board survives contact with a Tuesday.

The other thing I've noticed is that when your "system" is just a text file, you actually think about what matters. You can't hide low-priority tasks behind 47 nested sub-pages. The list is right there. Pick something. Do it. Cross it off.

Am I saying you should delete all your apps? No. Use whatever works. But if you're constantly tweaking your productivity setup, that's a sign. The setup is the problem, not the solution.

Sometimes the dumbest solution is the right one.

I'm writing this from a coffee shop on my laptop. My today.txt has four things on it. I've done two. Life is good.
